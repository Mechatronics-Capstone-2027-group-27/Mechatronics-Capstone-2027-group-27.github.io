## Preliminary Design Presentation (MTE 481)

**Team:** Group 27 · SupraNimbōs
**Presenters:** Ethan Catz, Ian Macpherson, David Makarczyk, Nitya Singh, Matthew Zelenka

We presented our preliminary design for a **balloon-lifted high-altitude gliding rocket launch system** to the MTE 481 teaching team. This was our first formal design review and closes out the concept phase of the project.

### What we presented

**Need and problem definition**
Free high-altitude balloons go where the wind takes them, which makes payload recovery slow and uncertain. We defined the need around landing a payload near a chosen site, then set out our objectives, constraints and criteria in measurable terms. The one pass/fail constraint is that the payload survives.

**Alternative designs and selection**
We compared nine launch concepts:
- ground-launched rocket
- railgun accelerator
- free high-altitude balloon
- control moment gyroscopes
- cold gas thrusters
- electrohydrodynamic thrusters
- sails
- dual balloon
- balloon-launched glider

We scored all nine on cost, weight, payload capacity, vertical and horizontal precision, recovery radius, time to target and ETA consistency. The two leaders were close, so we weighted the criteria toward the need: horizontal precision at 25% and recovery radius at 15%. We also ran a sensitivity check. The **balloon-launched glider** came out on top because it can fly itself back to a chosen landing site rather than drifting.

**Patent review and prior art**
We presented three relevant patents: Stratodynamics HiDRON, NOAA HORUS, Return Glider Radiosonde. 
We also showed related prior art: NASA's Towed Glider Air-Launch System, Stofiel Aerospace's balloon-based launchers, and Bell's 1907 Cygnet tetrahedral kite as an early example of towed lift with a planned release. For each, we covered how it shapes our design.

**Project timeline**
We laid out the plan from now to the symposium. The critical path runs through six phases:
1. Detailed Design and Analysis
2. Long-Lead Procurement
3. Fabrication
4. Integration and HIL Tests
5. Flight Test, with a backup flight window
6. Report and Symposium Prep

Flight approvals are treated as near-critical. Each major risk has a dated early-warning trigger and a fallback plan.

### Next steps
- Detailed design and glide/drift modelling
- Early procurement of long-lead parts (balloon, GPS, flight controller)
- Confirm Transport Canada and NAV CANADA requirements for the flight
